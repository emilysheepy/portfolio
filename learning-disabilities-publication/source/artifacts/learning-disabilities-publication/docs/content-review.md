## Learner-facing presentation

The publication is a standalone guide for managers. It must not describe itself as an adaptation, historical workshop, reading edition or editorial review. Keep provenance, historical verification flags and production notes here, not in visible copy, accessible labels, disclosures or metadata.

The revision removes the visible source markers, amber source rules, editorial warnings, review panel, “How to read this edition” section and adaptation explanations. It retains the established fonts, colours, title treatment, illustrations, decorative shapes, chapter structure and learning interactions.

The current revision brief authorizes targeted content corrections and a complete copyedit, while retaining the six-chapter structure and visual identity. Selected legal and scientific passages have now been checked against the authoritative information listed below. This is **not** a comprehensive legal review, clinical assessment or certification of current training content. Removing interface warnings is not verification.

## Verification and content-review register

### 1. Historical legal and scientific claims

**Original context:** Instructor guide dated 2011; job aid and decision tree contain 2012 citations.

**Open review:** Legal and scientific statements have not been checked against current legislation, case law or research. Obtain appropriate subject-matter review before using the guide as professional training. The learner-facing cleanup is not a legal or scientific update.

### 2. Dysgraphia source gap

**Original context:** Supplied explanations cover dyslexia, “Dycalculia” and auditory processing disorder; Table 1 of the Student Handout is missing.

**Open review:** Obtain an author-approved dysgraphia definition. The previous public placeholder, “No explanation in the supplied source,” was removed rather than inventing an explanation. Related supplied material mentions “expression – writing” and automated forms for handwriting difficulties.

### 3. Dyscalculia spelling

**Original wording:** “Dycalculia - impairs the ability to process numbers”.

**Open review:** Proposed spelling: “Dyscalculia”. No new definition or scientific assertion is authorized by the framing revision.

### 4. Causes and persistence

**Original wording:** “learning disabilities seem to result from neurological abnormalities which may be hereditary, head injury, or result from factors in the prenatal environment”.

**Open review:** Check the causal claims and terminology against current learning-disabilities guidance. The cause FAQ retains the source explanation; the attribution paragraph before the examples was replaced with a learning transition, not a new causal explanation. The grammatical issue “may be hereditary, head injury” also remains for review.

### 5. Charter applicability and relationship to the Human Rights Act

**Original wording:** The Charter protects against disability discrimination in employment; “The Human Rights Act enforces the Charter”.

**Open review:** Verify the scope of government action versus private employment and the relationship between statutes. Do not mistake removal of the public flag for legal approval.

### 6. Provincial and territorial scope

**Original context:** Federal legislation is discussed, but no provincial or territorial legal detail is supplied.

**Open review:** Confirm jurisdiction-specific content if expanded. The public guide retains the distinction between federally, provincially and territorially regulated employers and directs readers to the applicable legislation without inventing provincial rules.

### 7. Canadian Human Rights Code / Act

**Original policy wording:** “Canadian Human Rights Code” and “Section 10 of the Canadian Human Rights Code”.

**Open review:** The federal statute is the Canadian Human Rights Act. “Code” is used in some provincial statutes. Verify the statutory and section references before reusing the policy wording.

### 8. Bona fide justification abbreviation

**Original wording:** “bona fide justification (BOF)”.

**Open review:** Confirm an appropriate abbreviation or omit it. The original review proposed “BFJ”; do not treat that proposal as authoritative.

### 9. Denied-request criteria numbering

**Original numbering:** 1, 3, 4.

**Open review:** Determine whether the numbering alone is wrong or substantive content is missing. The phrase “(bona fide occupational )requirement” also needs a mechanical correction. No additional legal criterion has been invented.

### 10. Incomplete essential-requirement definition

**Original wording:** “an essential requirement is a [process, that cannot be altered without [compromising] the objective of the task”.

**Disposition:** Removed the incomplete glossary entry and its public draft label. The complete supplied BFOR definition and job-analysis explanations remain. Obtain an approved complete definition before adding a separate entry.

### 11. Accommodation-selection questions

**Original wording:** “Is so costly …”, “negatively effect coworkers”, “Will an accommodation has any union-related implications”.

**Open review:** Proposed corrections: “Is it so costly …”, “negatively affect coworkers”, “Will an accommodation have any union-related implications”. These proposals do not change the selection criteria.

### 12. Human Right Act / undue hardship

**Original wording:** “The Human Right Act specifies …”.

**Open review:** Proposed statute name: “The Canadian Human Rights Act”. Confirm the discussion of health, safety and cost, qualification, and the airline-pilot example with a legal professional.

### 13. Terminology

**Original terms:** “learning disorder”, “special needs”, “motormouth”, “dyslexic”.

**Open review:** Review terminology without changing substantive examples silently. The last two labels appear as examples of wording managers should avoid; they are not recommended descriptions.

### 14. Jeremiah example

**Original assessment note:** “Recognizes that the essential requirement of Joe’s position is to participate in an approved professional development coursel.”

**Open review:** This belongs to Jeremiah's scenario; proposed correction is “Jeremiah's position … course.” The assessment note is not part of the learner-facing scenario. The source's visual-processing aside beside an auditory-processing example also warrants review.

### 15. Jane and George case-study handout

**Source gap:** The separate participant case study is missing.

**Disposition:** Retain only the supplied paragraph and presentation prompts about Jane. Do not invent her job, history or George's actions.

### 16. Resource currency

**Original context:** Resource URLs were retrieved in 2012.

**Open review:** Check organization descriptions, funding statements, services and addresses. The public organization overview remains; historical dates, citations and review warnings have moved here.
