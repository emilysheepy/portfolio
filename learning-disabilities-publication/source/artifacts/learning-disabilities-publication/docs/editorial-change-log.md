# Editorial change log

## Scope

This revision follows the supplied revision brief for the six-chapter publication. It is a refinement of the existing application, not a rebuild. The knowledge check, original artwork, font families, colour palette and copyright notice are unchanged.

The table records significant changes; quoted excerpts are used where a full paragraph would repeat the same correction. Minor punctuation and sentence-case changes are grouped. The three Najeeb sample responses and their accompanying commentary retain their exact wording and order; a remaining commentary issue is recorded in `content-review.md`, not silently rewritten.

## Exact case-study replacements

### Chapter 4 — Jane

**Original**

> These problems indicate that Jane has difficulty processing and using information that she hears.

**Revised**

> Jane appears to be having difficulty following verbal instructions and recording information accurately. There are several possible explanations, including auditory processing difficulties, hearing loss, attention or memory difficulties, or the way instructions are communicated. Rather than making assumptions about the cause, George should discuss the specific workplace challenges with Jane and work with her to identify appropriate supports.

**Reason:** Exact replacement supplied in the brief. Distinguishes observations from a diagnosis and focuses on collaborative support.

### Chapter 4 — Opening a conversation

**Original**

> When you have noticed problematic behaviours in an employee which may indicate a learning disorder, you need to recognize that you may need to raise sensitive issues to help that employee perform their best.

**Revised**

> When you notice that an employee is having difficulty with particular job tasks, begin by identifying the specific challenges and discussing them with the employee. Avoid assuming that the difficulties are caused by a learning disability or another medical condition.

**Reason:** Exact replacement supplied in the brief. Removes stigmatizing framing and assumptions about the cause.
